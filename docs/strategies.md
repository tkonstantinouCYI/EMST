# Bidding strategies

Strategies generate offers; the market engines determine acceptance, dispatch
and settlement. They do not change market rules. This guide describes the public
`Market` API and the current implementation, including its limitations.

## Available strategies and selection

```python
from emst import Market, Strategy, ThermalState, StorageState

print(Market.available_strategies())
```

| Technology | Available strategies | Default when none is selected |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal | `marginal_cost`, `fixed_markup` | `marginal_cost` |
| RES | `forecast_offer` | `forecast_offer` |
| Supplier | `forecast_demand`, `price_responsive` | `forecast_demand` |
| Storage | `fixed_circular`, `forecast_arbitrage` | `fixed_circular` (idle without a profile) |

Select an owner's default per technology when registering the participant:

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant(
    "producer",
    strategies={"thermal": Strategy("fixed_markup", {"markup_eur_mwh": 10})},
)
```

An asset's explicit `strategy` overrides its owner's default. Asset calls accept
either a name plus `strategy_parameters`, or a `Strategy(name, parameters)`
instance. When supplying an instance, put the parameters inside that instance:
the separate `strategy_parameters` argument does not override it. The strategy
registry validates names against technology; parameter dictionaries do not have
a strict schema, and unknown keys can be ignored. Use the exact keys below.

The following examples are independent snippets: each creates its own market
and asset. They demonstrate configuration, not a complete runnable system.
Actual load/RES series are supplied for subsequent delivery and settlement;
the DAM strategies use their forecasts.

## Thermal: `marginal_cost`

The DAM sell price is the asset's `marginal_cost`, in EUR/MWh. For each half-hour,
the offered quantity is `capacity_mw * 0.5 - forward_sale_mwh`.

| Strategy parameter | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| None | — | Cost is supplied through the asset's required `marginal_cost` argument. |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("generator")
market.add_thermal(
    "unit", "generator", capacity_mw=100, minimum_mw=20,
    marginal_cost=70,
    initial_state=ThermalState("dispatch", 50, 120),
    strategy="marginal_cost",
)
```

This unit offers up to 50 MWh per MTU at EUR70/MWh before forward allocations.
The strategy does not calculate fuel costs, efficiencies, carbon costs or
opportunity costs: supply the marginal cost yourself. It does not generate a
multi-step cost curve or recover startup costs through its DAM price. Thermal
commitment, minimum output, ramps and reserve headroom are handled by ISP, not
by this DAM offer-generation rule.

## Thermal: `fixed_markup`

The quantity is the same as `marginal_cost`; the DAM price is
`marginal_cost + markup_eur_mwh`.

| Strategy parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---:|
| `markup_eur_mwh` | EUR/MWh | `0` |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("generator")
market.add_thermal(
    "unit", "generator", capacity_mw=100, minimum_mw=20,
    marginal_cost=70,
    initial_state=ThermalState("dispatch", 50, 120),
    strategy="fixed_markup",
    strategy_parameters={"markup_eur_mwh": 15},
)
```

This unit offers at EUR85/MWh. The markup is constant across the day and does
not adapt to demand, competitors, scarcity or realised profits. It is not an
optimisation of market power. Omitting the markup gives the same price as
`marginal_cost`.

## RES: `forecast_offer`

Offers `generation_forecast_mw * 0.5 - forward_sale_mwh` per MTU, at one configured
sell price. The forecast is an input, not a forecast generated by the strategy.

| Strategy parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `price_eur_mwh` | EUR/MWh | Ruleset `dam_price_floor` (currently `0`) |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("renewable_owner")
market.add_res(
    "portfolio", "renewable_owner", capacity_mw=30,
    generation_forecast=[20.0] * 48,
    actual_generation=[18.0] * 48,
    strategy="forecast_offer",
    strategy_parameters={"price_eur_mwh": 0},
)
```

The portfolio offers 10 MWh per MTU at EUR0/MWh. Actual generation is not used
to improve its bid forecast. The strategy does not optimise forecast risk,
curtailment exposure or forward/DAM/balancing revenues jointly. `grant_res`,
`actual_generation_kind` and `energy_down_price` are separate asset inputs,
not strategy parameters. RES capacity participation remains unsupported.

## Supplier: `forecast_demand`

Submits `demand_forecast_mw * 0.5 - forward_purchase_mwh` per MTU as a buy order.

| Strategy parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `price_eur_mwh` | EUR/MWh purchase limit | Ruleset `dam_price_cap` (currently `1000`) |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("supplier")
market.add_supplier(
    "customers", "supplier",
    demand_forecast=[40.0] * 48,
    actual_consumption=[42.0] * 48,
    strategy="forecast_demand",
)
```

This submits 20 MWh per MTU at the default purchase limit. The bid price is not
the price paid: accepted energy pays the DAM clearing price. A high limit tends
to prioritise acceptance, but does not guarantee it if supply is insufficient.
Forward purchases reduce the auction quantity and are settled separately.

## Supplier: `price_responsive`

**Currently the same implementation as `forecast_demand`.** The name provides
no extra response model; set a lower `price_eur_mwh` to impose a willingness-to-pay
limit. Without an explicit price, it also defaults to the ruleset price cap.

| Strategy parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `price_eur_mwh` | EUR/MWh purchase limit | Ruleset `dam_price_cap` (currently `1000`) |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("supplier")
market.add_supplier(
    "flexible_customers", "supplier",
    demand_forecast=[40.0] * 48,
    actual_consumption=[42.0] * 48,
    strategy="price_responsive",
    strategy_parameters={"price_eur_mwh": 150},
)
```

The requested quantity is still 20 MWh per MTU. It can be partially or entirely
unaccepted depending on the auction. There is no demand-elasticity curve, load
shifting, rebound, customer utility optimisation or automatic physical demand
reduction. Unaccepted DAM demand does **not** reduce `actual_consumption`:
the supplied actual series remains the settlement meter and can create imbalance.
At the same price parameter, the two supplier strategies generate identical bids.

## Storage: shared settings

Both storage strategies generate separate DAM buy and sell orders. Technical
inputs belong to `add_storage`, not `strategy_parameters`:

| Asset input | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `power_mw` | MW charge/discharge limit | Required |
| `capacity_mwh` | MWh energy capacity | Required |
| `initial_state` | `StorageState(power_mw, soc_mwh)` | Required |
| `efficiency_charge` | Fraction | `0.95` |
| `efficiency_discharge` | Fraction | `0.95` |
| `ramp_mw_hour` | MW/hour | `10000` |
| `degradation_cost` | EUR/MWh throughput | `0` |
| `reserve_cost` | EUR/MW/MTU | `1` |
| `reserve_prices` | Product/direction overrides | Defaults to `reserve_cost` |

Both accept these strategy parameters:

| Parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `buy_price` | EUR/MWh purchase limit | Ruleset `dam_price_cap` (currently `1000`) |
| `sell_price` | EUR/MWh sale limit | Ruleset `dam_price_floor` (currently `0`) |
| `balancing_up_price` | EUR/MWh | `100` |
| `balancing_down_price` | EUR/MWh | `0` |

Buy/sell price limits are fixed scalar values across the day; they are not the
forecast price array and are not necessarily the settlement prices. Upward
balancing offer price must be at least downward price. Balancing participation
is independently controlled by the ruleset and defaults to **false** for both
storage energy and capacity. Setting offer prices does not enable participation.

With the default `storage.dam.circular_blocks=True`, all nonzero legs of an
asset's daily profile form one linked, all-or-nothing circular block. Clearing
can reject it. All submitted profiles and accepted schedules are checked for
SOC, power and ramp feasibility. If circular blocks are disabled, legs can clear
independently; an infeasible resulting storage schedule causes an error rather
than being silently repaired.

## Storage: `fixed_circular`

Use a user-supplied energy profile rather than optimising it against prices.

| Additional strategy parameter | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `buy_mwh` | Nonnegative MWh per MTU, 48 values | All zeros |
| `sell_mwh` | Nonnegative MWh per MTU, 48 values | All zeros |

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("storage_owner")
market.add_storage(
    "battery", "storage_owner", power_mw=10, capacity_mwh=40,
    initial_state=StorageState(power_mw=0, soc_mwh=20),
    strategy="fixed_circular",
    strategy_parameters={
        "buy_mwh": [2.0 if t in (10, 11) else 0 for t in range(48)],
        "sell_mwh": [1.805 if t in (34, 35) else 0 for t in range(48)],
        "buy_price": 100,
        "sell_price": 150,
    },
)
```

The full profile buys 4 MWh and sells 3.61 MWh, returning SOC to its initial
value at the default efficiencies if accepted and followed. A 2 MWh purchase
over a half-hour corresponds to 4 MW charging. Buying and selling simultaneously
within an MTU is rejected. Profiles must be feasible from the supplied previous
power and SOC even before auction acceptance.

The name refers to the linked order structure: **this strategy does not enforce
ending SOC equal to starting SOC**. You must design the desired endpoint in the
profiles. `terminal_soc_mwh` has no effect on `fixed_circular`. With both arrays
omitted, the strategy is idle. It does not choose trading hours, optimise profit
or revise the profile after observing clearing prices. Permissive buy/sell limits
can accept an energy-loss cycle even at flat prices; acceptance is not proof
of profitable arbitrage.

## Storage: `forecast_arbitrage`

Optimises a daily charge/discharge profile against an **external price forecast**
before submitting it to DAM. It minimises forecast purchase cost minus sales
revenue plus degradation cost on charged and discharged energy.

| Additional strategy parameter/input | Unit | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `terminal_soc_mwh` in `strategy_parameters` | MWh | Initial SOC |
| `price_forecast` passed to `market.run` | EUR/MWh per MTU, 48 values | Required; missing input raises an error |

The optimisation enforces power/energy capacity, ramps from the initial power,
charge/discharge efficiencies, exclusive charge/discharge mode and terminal SOC.
An unreachable terminal target produces an infeasible optimisation. `buy_mwh`
and `sell_mwh` parameters are not used by this strategy.

```python
market = Market()
market.add_participant("storage_owner")
market.add_storage(
    "battery", "storage_owner", power_mw=10, capacity_mwh=40,
    initial_state=StorageState(power_mw=0, soc_mwh=20),
    efficiency_charge=0.95, efficiency_discharge=0.95,
    degradation_cost=2,
    strategy="forecast_arbitrage",
    strategy_parameters={"terminal_soc_mwh": 20},
)
price_forecast = [50.0 if 10 <= t < 16 else 150.0 for t in range(48)]
```

Add the rest of your system (including at least one supplier), then pass
`price_forecast=price_forecast` to `market.run(...)`, together with its other
scenario inputs. The forecast is market-level and shared by storage assets;
it is not passed to `add_storage`. For example, continue the snippet above with
this small synthetic system and run (the historical prices here are fictional):

```python
market.add_participant("generator")
market.add_participant("supplier")
market.add_thermal(
    "unit", "generator", capacity_mw=100, minimum_mw=10,
    marginal_cost=70, initial_state=ThermalState("dispatch", 50, 120),
)
market.add_supplier(
    "load", "supplier", demand_forecast=[40.0] * 48,
    actual_consumption=[40.0] * 48,
)
history = {t: [[80.0] * 6 for _ in range(3)] for t in range(48)}
results = market.run(
    time_index=market.time_index("2026-11-05"),
    price_forecast=price_forecast,
    historical_rtbm_prices=history,
)
results.save_readable("output/strategy-guide-example")
```

The strategy receives no future cleared prices or actual demand/RES. It is a
price-taking deterministic profile optimiser, with no forecast uncertainty,
competitor response or joint reserve/balancing revenue optimisation. The later
DAM may reject its block, and realised prices and profits may differ from the
forecast. Terminal SOC constrains the proposed profile, not an unconditional
end-of-day realised SOC when balancing changes dispatch or the block is rejected.
There is no next-day lookahead or terminal value for future trading days.

## ISP/RTBM offers, forward contracts and current boundaries

- **Thermal:** `energy_up_price` and `energy_down_price` are separate
  `add_thermal` inputs for ISP energy offers. Each defaults to the strategy's
  base price (cost or cost plus markup). Both must be finite, with up >= down.
  `reserve_cost` defaults to EUR2/MW/MTU, with `reserve_prices` available for
  product/direction overrides. There is no separate named balancing strategy.
- **Thermal RTBM limitation:** its dispatch objective currently uses the
  strategy-generated base energy price plus the configured schedule-deviation
  coefficient, rather than the independent ISP up/down overrides. Do not assume
  that changing only those overrides changes thermal RTBM dispatch economics.
  RTBM rows export its effective base-price-plus/minus-coefficient quotes;
  the independent directional overrides apply to ISP, not those RTBM quotes.
- **RES:** `energy_down_price` is an asset input, default EUR0/MWh. Downward
  energy participation and capacity eligibility follow the ruleset; RES capacity
  participation is currently unsupported. No upward RES bidding strategy exists.
- **Storage:** directional balancing prices are the shared strategy parameters
  above; reserve prices are separate asset inputs. Eligibility, reserve headroom
  in ISP and physical SOC/power constraints still apply.
- **Suppliers:** no named balancing-offer strategy is implemented. Actual
  consumption differences are handled through imbalance settlement.
- **Forward contracts:** supplied through `Forward` inputs. No strategy selects
  contract volumes, counterparties or prices, and there is no joint forward/DAM
  portfolio optimiser.
- All seven strategies use fixed rules or deterministic optimisation. No
  reinforcement learning, adaptive agents, automatic forecast production,
  strategic equilibrium solver or intraday bidding strategy is implemented.

Settlement cashflows, including UA1 and UA2, are calculated from cleared and
delivered results; strategies do not set settlement prices or uplift allocations.
See the [API guide](api.md) for units, eligibility and result exports.
